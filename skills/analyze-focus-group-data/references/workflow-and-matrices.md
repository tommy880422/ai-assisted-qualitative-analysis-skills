# Analysis Workflow and Matrices

## Group-Level Memo

Write one memo per group before combining codes:

- design category, composition, mode, setting, moderator, and deviations;
- discussion trajectory and key turning points;
- central positions and shared terms;
- disagreements, qualifications, alliances, and silences;
- intense, specific, or elaborated episodes;
- effects of questions, stimuli, activities, or moderator behavior;
- unexpected findings and missing expected topics;
- initial comparisons and follow-up needs;
- provisional interpretation and counter-interpretation.

## Comparing Without Vote Counting

Consider:

- **Extensiveness:** how broadly an issue appears across groups or speakers;
- **Frequency:** how often it recurs in the corpus;
- **Intensity:** emotional, rhetorical, or interactional force;
- **Specificity:** detail, examples, and consequences;
- **Internal consistency:** whether a position is maintained, qualified, or changed;
- **Interactional uptake:** whether others elaborate, resist, ignore, or transform it;
- **Context:** which composition, setting, question, or stimulus enabled it.

Use these as interpretive dimensions. Do not combine them into an unvalidated score.

## From Pattern to Finding

For every finding, write:

1. concise interpretive claim;
2. relevant groups and contexts;
3. interactional process that supports the claim;
4. representative evidence;
5. variation and counter-evidence;
6. relationship to research question and theory;
7. claim limit and plausible alternative.
