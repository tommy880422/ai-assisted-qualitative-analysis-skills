## 1. Positioning JTBD in a thesis

State:

- why JTBD fits the phenomenon and research question;
- which formulation is primary;
- which constructs are included and excluded;
- whether JTBD is a sensitizing lens, analytic framework, explanatory proposition, or applied process;
- how adjacent approaches are used without being conflated;
- which empirical or theoretical gap the study addresses;
- what level of claim the design can support.

Use careful language. The supplied literature is dominated by practitioner books and management articles, with fewer peer-reviewed empirical applications. Present commercial cases and author-reported success claims as evidence of application or source claims, not as conclusive independent validation.

## 2. Theory chapter structure

Use this sequence:

1. **Problem with product/person segmentation:** Explain why the study needs an episode- and circumstance-sensitive lens.
2. **Shared JTBD proposition:** Define the minimal common core.
3. **Variant genealogy:** Distinguish Christensen, Ulwick/ODI, Wunker, and Anthony; identify I-Corps and disruption as adjacent.
4. **Selected formulation:** Explain why it best fits the research question.
5. **Construct definitions:** Define job/progress, circumstance, dimensions, solutions, forces, outcomes, and stakeholder roles as applicable.
6. **Proposed relationships:** State mechanism expectations or sensitizing questions without overstating them.
7. **Boundary conditions:** Identify actor, setting, episode, institutional constraint, and time horizon.
8. **Conceptual framework:** Show only relationships the study will examine.
9. **Research questions/propositions:** Derive them from the framework.

### Variant-rationale sentence pattern

> This study adopts [formulation] as its primary JTBD formulation because the research question concerns [phenomenon]. It uses [secondary formulation] only for [bounded purpose] and does not treat [adjacent approach] as interchangeable with JTBD.

## 3. Methods chapter structure

Report:

1. Research paradigm/logic and qualitative, mixed, or longitudinal design.
2. Role of theory in sampling, questioning, and coding.
3. Setting and case boundaries.
4. Unit of analysis: job episode and any embedded stakeholder/system levels.
5. Sampling strategy, contrast cells, recruitment, inclusion, and stopping review.
6. Participant/site characteristics relevant to interpretation.
7. Instrument development, pilot, and version changes.
8. Fieldwork procedure and interviewer role.
9. Recording, transcription, translation, and data management.
10. Analysis stages, codebook development, displays, software, and team process.
11. Negative-case, rival-explanation, triangulation, and reflexivity procedures.
12. Quantitative procedures, if any, including item development and score transformation.
13. Ethics approval/consent as applicable.
14. Deviations from protocol.

Do not state only that interviews were “analyzed using JTBD.” Describe how episodes were bounded, coded, compared, and converted into job claims.

## 4. Findings structures

### 4.1 Job-centered structure

For each candidate job:

1. Job claim.
2. Circumstances and participants/cases represented.
3. Functional, emotional, and social dimensions.
4. Current solutions and alternatives.
5. Success criteria/outcomes.
6. Variation and boundary conditions.
7. Negative case.

Use when the contribution is a set of job patterns.

### 4.2 Process-centered structure

Organize by:

- background/current approach;
- trigger/struggling moment;
- passive and active search;
- choice/non-choice;
- Big Hire;
- Little Hire/repeated use;
- outcome and revision.

Show push, pull, habit/inertia, and anxiety at the stage where each operated.

### 4.3 Stakeholder-centered structure

1. Within-role jobs.
2. Within-role success criteria and forces.
3. Cross-role dependencies.
4. Aligned jobs.
5. Conflicting jobs and cost/value shifts.
6. System-level consequences.

Do not collapse patient, clinician, manager, payer, and implementer into an average stakeholder.

### 4.4 ODI-centered structure

Report:

- executor and core job;
- job map;
- outcome-statement development;
- survey sample and transformation;
- importance and satisfaction distributions;
- opportunity scores with uncertainty/sensitivity;
- segments and validation if performed;
- qualitative explanations and negative cases;
- implications separated from findings.

## 5. Discussion structure

Move in this order:

1. Answer each research question directly.
2. State the main empirical pattern and its scope.
3. Compare with the selected JTBD formulation.
4. Explain where data extend, refine, contradict, or bound the formulation.
5. Compare plausible rival explanations or adjacent theories.
6. State methodological contribution, if any.
7. State practical implications that follow from evidence.
8. State limitations and transfer conditions.
9. Propose research that would test unresolved mechanisms.

Separate:

- empirical finding;
- theoretical interpretation;
- design or management recommendation.

Avoid changing a participant's desired progress into a product prescription without an explicit inference step.

## 6. Quality criteria

### Credibility

- Use concrete episodes and context-rich evidence.
- Triangulate statements with observation, artifacts, records, or follow-up where useful.
- Include negative cases and contradictions.
- Use participant feedback as a targeted check, not a vote on the analysis.

### Dependability

- Version instruments and codebooks.
- Keep decision, merge/split, and protocol-deviation logs.
- Preserve case summaries and matrix snapshots.

### Confirmability

- Link every claim to source locators.
- Record researcher assumptions and product/theory interests.
- Preserve rival explanations and confidence judgments.

### Transferability

- Describe circumstances, roles, constraints, and setting.
- Specify which features are likely relevant elsewhere and which are local.
- Avoid statistical representativeness claims from purposive samples.

### Construct integrity

- Keep job, circumstance, outcome, solution, activity, and stakeholder separate.
- State the JTBD variant and do not drift between definitions.
- Show how raw evidence becomes a job statement.

### Consequential/ethical quality

- Examine whose job is privileged.
- Identify who receives value and who bears cost or risk.
- Avoid recommendations that intensify surveillance, exclusion, burden, or clinical risk.

## 6A. Reporting standards (COREQ / SRQR)

**Not in the supplied corpus.** COREQ and SRQR are external instruments. Obtain the actual checklist and complete it against the published item wording; the summary below is a routing aid, not a substitute.

- **COREQ** — Tong, A., Sainsbury, P., & Craig, J. (2007). Consolidated criteria for reporting qualitative research (COREQ): a 32-item checklist for interviews and focus groups. *International Journal for Quality in Health Care, 19*(6), 349–357. Three domains: research team and reflexivity; study design; analysis and findings. This is the one most nursing and health-services journals request for interview and focus-group studies.
- **SRQR** — O'Brien, B. C., Harris, I. B., Beckman, T. J., Reed, D. A., & Cook, D. A. (2014). Standards for reporting qualitative research: a synthesis of recommendations. *Academic Medicine, 89*(9), 1245–1251. 21 items, broader in scope and not restricted to interviews.

### Where this skill's artifacts land

| Reporting domain | Artifact produced by this skill |
|---|---|
| Research team, reflexivity, relationship with participants | Reflexive memos (analysis section 11), positionality heading in the design canvas |
| Theoretical framework and methodological orientation | Primary formulation and rationale; the variant-rationale sentence pattern in section 2 |
| Participant selection, setting, non-participation | Sampling matrix and contrast cells; recruitment and consent section of the canvas |
| Data collection, interview guide, pilot, repeat interviews, saturation | Instrument versions, pilot checklist, stopping review |
| Data analysis, number of coders, derivation of themes, software | Codebook versions, analysis stages, team-coding log |
| Reporting: quotations, participant numbers, consistency, clarity of themes | Claim-evidence table, findings package, negative-case log |

### The four COREQ items JTBD studies characteristically fail

1. **Methodological orientation.** "Interviews were analysed using JTBD" names a lens, not a method. State the actual analytic procedure and cite it, then state which JTBD constructs organized it.
2. **Derivation of themes.** JTBD codes are theory-derived, not emergent. Say so explicitly rather than implying inductive derivation.
3. **Saturation.** Do not claim it by default. Define what kind of adequacy is claimed, for which construct, and on what basis.
4. **Participant checking.** Report it as a targeted check on specific interpretations, not as participants validating the job statement. A validated job statement is not thereby objective.

### What COREQ and SRQR do not cover

Completing either checklist does not discharge the JTBD-specific reporting this skill requires. Report additionally:

- which formulation is primary, and the boundary if a secondary one is used;
- operational definitions distinguishing job, circumstance, need, outcome, solution, and activity;
- how job episodes were bounded, and how many episodes each participant contributed;
- for any ODI phase: outcome-statement development, scale and transformation, threshold, and sensitivity.

## 7. Evidence and citation rules

### 7.1 Claim-evidence table

Maintain:

| Claim ID | Claim | Evidence type | Case/episode IDs | Source locators | Negative evidence | Confidence | Intended section |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### 7.2 Source hierarchy

Distinguish:

- primary conceptual source;
- practitioner method source;
- peer-reviewed empirical application;
- teaching handbook;
- adjacent theory source;
- secondary summary.

Do not cite a secondary briefing for a definition available in the primary source merely for convenience. Do cite the secondary source when discussing its specific extension or synthesis.

### 7.3 Quotation practice

- Quote participants only when wording itself supports interpretation.
- Use short excerpts with enough context.
- Remove or mask identifying detail without altering meaning.
- Paraphrase copyrighted literature and quote sparingly.
- Verify page numbers against the actual edition/PDF.
- Do not cite PDF-extraction line numbers in a thesis; use published or PDF page numbers consistently and explain when pagination differs.

### 7.4 Claim labels

Use explicit attribution:

- “Christensen and colleagues conceptualize…”
- “Ulwick's ODI method proposes…”
- “The present participants described…”
- “The analysis interprets this pattern as…”
- “This implication remains to be tested…”

## 8. Limitations library

Adapt only applicable limitations:

### Theory and construct

- JTBD contains multiple formulations, and the selected operationalization may foreground some mechanisms while excluding others.
- Retrospective progress narratives may reflect post-hoc coherence rather than the full decision process.
- The metaphor of hiring/firing may overstate deliberateness in constrained or mandated settings.
- Claims of job stability may not hold across cultural, policy, technological, or life-course change.

### Sampling

- Purposive contrast sampling supports analytic depth but not prevalence estimates.
- Hard-to-reach nonconsumers or failed users may be underrepresented.
- Role-based samples may miss invisible informal caregivers, brokers, or affected parties.

### Data generation

- Recall windows, social desirability, and interviewer prompts can reshape accounts.
- Observation may capture action without private motivation.
- Focus-group interaction may suppress sensitive disagreement.
- Translation can alter distinctions among job, need, goal, and emotion.

### Analysis

- Theory-led codes can cause premature closure or circular confirmation.
- Job statements condense heterogeneous episodes and may obscure minority patterns.
- Researcher familiarity with a proposed solution can bias interpretation.

### ODI/quantitative

- Outcome wording and scale transformation materially affect scores.
- The threshold of 10 is method-specific and not an inferential cutoff.
- Aggregate importance and satisfaction can conceal heterogeneous segments.
- Data-driven clusters may be unstable or sample-specific.
- Commercial case success claims in the source corpus are not independent effectiveness trials.

### Application

- A well-described job does not demonstrate that a proposed intervention is feasible, safe, effective, equitable, or disruptive.
- Stakeholder alignment in one site may depend on local financing, policy, staffing, or workflow.

## 9. Audit trail

Maintain:

1. Source ledger and edition/page notes.
2. Research-question and construct versions.
3. Sampling matrix and recruitment outcomes.
4. Consent and instrument versions.
5. Fieldwork/debrief memos.
6. Transcript correction and translation decisions.
7. Codebook versions.
8. Case summaries and episode-boundary decisions.
9. Code merge/split and job-statement decision logs.
10. Negative-case and rival-explanation log.
11. Quantitative cleaning, transformations, scripts, and sensitivity runs.
12. Claim-evidence table.
13. Deviations and rationale.
14. Researcher reflexive memos.

Exclude identifiable keys from the analytic audit package.
