## 1. Instrument principles

Start with a concrete episode, not an abstract opinion. Ask short, neutral questions and follow the participant's chronology. Probe for observable detail without cross-examining.

Use these safeguards:

- Ask about the last or most recent relevant episode.
- Distinguish what happened from what usually happens.
- Ask for artifacts or environmental details only with consent.
- Revisit contradictions as differences between moments, not as participant failure.
- Ask about action and non-action, including delay and doing nothing.
- Delay product concepts and value propositions until the current story is complete.
- Do not teach JTBD vocabulary to participants.
- Record interviewer prompts that may have shaped a response.

## 2. Recruitment screener

Adapt these items to the setting:

1. Have you personally faced [focal situation] within [recall window]?
2. Did you participate in deciding what to do?
3. Which best describes the episode: adopted/switched, considered but did not change, stopped using, improvised a workaround, or did nothing?
4. Which role did you have: executor/user, buyer, gatekeeper, provider/implementer, supporter, or other affected party?
5. Can you discuss one specific episode in detail?
6. Did anyone else strongly influence the decision or use?
7. Are there privacy, employment, or care-related constraints on participation?

Use the responses to fill purposive contrast cells. Do not select only enthusiastic users.

## 3. Switching or critical-incident interview

### Opening

- Please think of the most recent time you changed, began, stopped, or seriously considered changing how you handled [situation].
- Where should the story begin for it to make sense?

### Before the trigger

- How had you been handling this before?
- What was acceptable about that approach?
- What was difficult, risky, costly, embarrassing, or tiring?
- Was there a workaround or something you simply tolerated?
- Who else was involved or affected?

### Trigger or struggling moment

- What happened that made this episode different from the usual situation?
- Was there one event or a gradual accumulation?
- What did you hope would be different afterward?
- Why did this matter at that particular time?

### Search and alternatives

- What options came to mind first?
- What else could you have done, including waiting or doing nothing?
- Where did you look for information or reassurance?
- Which people, rules, resources, or constraints shaped the options?
- What did each option seem good or bad at doing?

### Forces

- What pushed you away from the old approach?
- What attracted you toward the new approach?
- What made staying with the old approach easier or more comfortable?
- What worried you about changing?
- What nearly stopped the decision?
- What reduced enough uncertainty for you to proceed—or not proceed?

### Decision and Big Hire

- When did this become a real decision rather than an idea?
- What trade-offs did you accept?
- Who had to agree, pay, approve, or help?
- What did you stop, reduce, or “fire” to make room for this option?

### Use and Little Hire

- Tell me about the first actual use.
- What happened compared with what you expected?
- Did you use it again? Why or why not?
- Did you adapt, combine, or bypass parts of the solution?
- What still required effort or another workaround?

### Desired progress and dimensions

- Looking back, what were you really trying to make possible?
- What practical result mattered?
- How did you hope to feel—or avoid feeling?
- Did it matter how others saw you or how you related to them?
- How would you recognize a fully successful outcome?

### Consequences and counterfactual

- What changed after the episode?
- What did not change?
- If the chosen option had not existed, what would you most likely have done?
- Under what circumstances would you make a different choice?

### Close

- What important part of the story have I missed?
- Is there an artifact, message, record, or place that would help us understand the episode, if you are comfortable sharing it?

## 4. Current-user and Little-Hire interview

Use when adoption is known but actual use is uncertain:

- What led to the original acquisition or enrollment?
- When was it last used? Reconstruct that use.
- What prompts use, and what prevents it?
- Which features or service elements are ignored, repurposed, or worked around?
- What alternatives are used in the same week or circumstance?
- What must be true for repeated use?
- When has the solution been available but not hired?
- What counts as a good-enough result?
- What would cause abandonment?

Compare stated value with behavioral traces where consent and access permit.

## 5. Nonconsumer or rejection interview

Do not assume nonconsumers have the same job but lack awareness.

- Tell me about the last time the relevant situation occurred.
- What did you do, if anything?
- Did the problem feel important enough to act on?
- Which solutions did you know about or consider?
- What made them unsuitable, inaccessible, unnecessary, or risky?
- What workaround or existing routine was good enough?
- What would have to change in the circumstance for action to become worthwhile?
- Which costs matter besides money?
- Who benefits or bears risk if you change?

Differentiate satisfied nonconsumption, constrained nonconsumption, active rejection, and deferred action.

## 6. Multi-stakeholder implementation interview

Interview each role separately before asking about alignment.

### Role and accountability

- What are you responsible for in this process?
- How is success judged for you?
- Which decisions can you make, and which require approval?

### Current workflow

- Walk me through the most recent relevant case.
- Where do handoffs occur?
- What do you create, receive, verify, or document?
- Where does the workflow break down or require improvisation?

### Stakeholder job

- What are you trying to make possible in this situation?
- What practical, emotional, professional, reputational, or financial outcomes matter?
- Which other role's actions help or obstruct this?

### Adoption and use

- What would you have to stop or change to adopt the proposed practice?
- What creates workload, clinical, legal, budget, identity, or career anxiety?
- What training, supervision, evidence, reimbursement, or local adaptation is necessary?
- What would make first adoption fail to become routine use?

### Tension and system effect

- Where do your success criteria conflict with another stakeholder's?
- Who carries cost while someone else receives value?
- What unintended consequences might appear?
- Which core function must not be lost during adaptation?

## 7. ODI job-map and outcome interview

Use only after defining the executor and provisional core functional job.

### Job-map prompts

- Before executing [job], what must be defined or planned?
- What information, people, or resources must be located?
- What must be prepared or set up?
- What must be confirmed before action?
- What is executed?
- What must be monitored?
- What is modified when conditions change?
- How is the job concluded, documented, or handed off?

Do not force all eight universal steps. Phrase each step as the goal being achieved, not the current product action.

### Outcome prompts

At each job step, ask:

- What makes this step take too long?
- What could go wrong, vary, or remain uncertain?
- What must be accurate, timely, complete, predictable, or low effort?
- What waste, risk, rework, or delay should be minimized?
- What tells you this step was successful?
- Under what condition is that criterion especially important?

Draft outcomes after the interview using:

> direction + metric + object of control + contextual clarifier

Return unclear wording for cognitive testing, not for participant approval of the whole theory.

## 8. Observation guide

Record four columns separately:

| Time/place | Observable action and environment | Participant explanation | Researcher interpretation/question |
|---|---|---|---|

Attend to:

- trigger and interruptions;
- physical and digital environment;
- people present and absent;
- sequence and handoffs;
- waiting, searching, checking, repeating, and correcting;
- unofficial tools, notes, spreadsheets, or messaging;
- product misuse or unusual use;
- workarounds and compensating behaviors;
- non-action or avoidance;
- emotional display without assigning motive;
- gaps between formal procedure and enacted practice;
- consequences for other stakeholders.

Ask short clarification questions after the action when interruption would distort it.

## 9. Diary protocol

Keep entries brief enough to sustain participation. Prompt after each focal episode:

1. Date, time, place, and who was present.
2. What were you trying to accomplish?
3. What triggered the episode?
4. What did you use or do?
5. What alternative did you consider?
6. What was hardest or uncertain?
7. What result did you get?
8. What will you do next time?
9. Optional artifact/photo/screenshot, with privacy check.

Use a follow-up interview to reconstruct selected entries and clarify variation. Do not infer that missing entries mean no job occurred.

## 10. Focus-group adaptation

> Use `conduct-focus-groups` to design and moderate the sessions and `analyze-focus-group-data` to analyze them. The steps below are only the JTBD-specific adaptation: protecting individual episode detail from group averaging.

Use focus groups to examine shared language, norms, collective workarounds, and disagreement. Protect individual episodes from group averaging:

1. Begin with silent individual recall and note-taking.
2. Invite each participant to narrate one episode.
3. Map similarities and differences in circumstances.
4. Ask where the group agrees, disagrees, hesitates, jokes, or corrects itself.
5. Separate consensus from conformity and dominant voices.
6. Do not vote a job statement into truth.
7. Follow up individually when a sensitive or complex switching story emerges.

## 11. I-Corps learning log

For each interview cycle, capture:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Hypothesis | What the team expected about stakeholder, job, barrier, or value |
| Evidence needed | What observation would support or challenge it |
| Participant role | Who was interviewed and why |
| Episode | Which concrete situation was discussed |
| Finding | What was actually learned |
| Disconfirming evidence | What did not fit the hypothesis |
| Decision | Preserve, revise, split, or reject the hypothesis |
| Next test | Who/what should be examined next |

Call a change an iteration when the core proposition remains and a pivot when a material assumption changes. Preserve the earlier version in the log.

## 12. Pilot and interviewer checklist

After a pilot, verify:

- The participant told a concrete episode within the planned time.
- The interviewer talked less than the participant.
- Questions did not reveal the preferred job or solution.
- The story includes before, trigger, search, choice/non-choice, use, and aftermath as relevant.
- Non-product alternatives and doing nothing were explored.
- Functional, emotional, and social evidence came from the episode, not abstract prompting alone.
- Other stakeholders and institutional constraints were captured.
- Contradictions and uncertainty remain visible.
- The instrument did not collect unnecessary identifying data.
- The resulting data can populate the case-evidence template.

Revise the order and probes, not the participant's answers. Document all material instrument changes and which cases used each version.
