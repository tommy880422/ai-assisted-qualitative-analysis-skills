# Apply Jobs-to-be-Done Theory

## Set the standard

Treat JTBD as a family of related formulations, not one settled protocol. Select and name the formulation before designing instruments or coding data. Preserve disagreements among formulations instead of silently combining them.

For thesis work, prioritize construct clarity, evidence traceability, rival explanations, research ethics, and defensible limits over consultancy-style certainty. Distinguish what a source argues, what participants report, what the researcher interprets, and what the data can support.

Match the user's language. Use academically natural Traditional Chinese when the user writes in Chinese; retain the English term at first use when translation could blur a construct.

## Execute the core workflow

### 1. Establish fit and scope

Identify:

1. The phenomenon and decision, adoption, change, or progress to explain.
2. The likely job executor, buyer, user, gatekeeper, implementer, and affected stakeholder.
3. The episode or circumstance in which the focal choice occurs.
4. The requested inference: description, interpretation, explanation, prioritization, design, or evaluation.
5. The available data and whether collection is prospective or already complete.

Use JTBD when the research asks why actors choose, switch, persist, improvise, adopt, reject, or repeatedly use solutions while trying to make progress in circumstances. Reconsider JTBD as the primary lens when the question concerns prevalence, causal effects of an intervention, broad attitudes without episodes, clinical efficacy, or organizational outcomes without an actor-level job mechanism.

### 2. Select one primary formulation

Choose the primary formulation from the JTBD family only:

| JTBD formulation | Primary construct | Best fit | Typical outputs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Christensen / *Competing Against Luck* | Progress sought in a circumstance | Choice, switching, adoption, persistence, experience | Job narrative, forces diagram, job spec |
| Ulwick / ODI | Stable functional job plus measurable desired outcomes | Opportunity prioritization and mixed-method innovation research | Job map, outcome statements, scores, segments |
| Wunker / Jobs Atlas | Jobs, drivers, approaches, pain points, success, obstacles, value, competition | Broad applied discovery and strategy | Jobs Atlas, prioritized opportunity areas |
| Anthony et al. | Fundamental problem in a circumstance plus objectives, barriers, alternatives, and quality | Early opportunity discovery | Job tree, observation findings, scoring sheet |

The following are **adjacent approaches, not JTBD formulations**. Never select one as the primary JTBD formulation; pair it with a formulation from the table above and report the two separately:

| Adjacent approach | What it contributes | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| I-Corps customer discovery | Testable stakeholder and value-proposition hypotheses; iteration/pivot records | A theory of the job; it structures learning cycles, not job constructs |
| Disruptive innovation theory | Account of entrant trajectories, business models, and incumbent response | Evidence that a job exists or that serving a job makes an innovation disruptive |
| Customer journey mapping | Description of interaction sequence and touchpoints | An explanation of the progress sought |

Use a secondary formulation only when it answers a distinct subquestion. State the boundary explicitly, for example: use Christensen to interpret switching narratives, then use ODI only in a separate survey phase to prioritize desired outcomes. Do not label I-Corps, disruption theory, a customer journey map, or a generic needs interview as JTBD by itself.

### 3. Build the construct map

Define before fieldwork or coding:

- **Job executor:** the actor trying to make progress; do not assume this is the purchaser. The term is formulation-specific: under Christensen it is the person seeking progress, whereas Ulwick applies it to whoever is targeted for value creation — end user, purchase decision maker, or a consumption-chain executor such as an installer. Define which sense the study uses.
- **Circumstance/episode:** the specific temporal, social, material, institutional, and life-stage context.
- **Desired progress or core functional job:** the change the actor seeks, stated without a product or feature.
- **Functional, emotional, and social dimensions:** distinguish dimensions of one job from unrelated jobs.
- **Current and competing solutions:** include products, services, people, self-made workarounds, delay, and doing nothing.
- **Choice or switching forces:** push of the current situation, pull of a new solution, habit/inertia, and anxiety/uncertainty.
- **Success criteria or desired outcomes:** the standards used to judge whether progress is achieved.
- **Big Hire and Little Hire:** distinguish acquisition/adoption from actual or repeated use when relevant.
- **Stakeholder jobs:** model each stakeholder's job separately before analyzing alignment or conflict.

Write provisional constructs as hypotheses. Do not convert participant wording directly into a final job statement without cross-case analysis.

### 4. Design an auditable study

Align research question, unit of analysis, sampling, data generation, and analysis. Use the job episode—not a demographic category or product—as the default unit of analysis.

Copy [jtbd-study-design-canvas.md](assets/jtbd-study-design-canvas.md) when the user needs a protocol, proposal, or thesis design scaffold.

Sample purposively for information-rich contrasts: successful and failed episodes, switchers and non-switchers, adopters and rejecters, frequent and abandoned use, current customers and nonconsumers, and materially different circumstances. In multi-actor systems, sample each consequential role.

Choose methods by evidence need:

- Use critical-incident or switching interviews for decisions and change over time.
- Use observation or diaries for tacit workarounds, routine behavior, and Big-Hire/Little-Hire gaps.
- Use focus groups only when interaction and shared norms are analytically useful; protect individual episode detail.
- Use surveys after qualitative construct development when estimating importance, satisfaction, prevalence, or segments.
- Use documents and usage records to triangulate claims, not to infer motives automatically.

Specify consent, privacy, recording, transcription, translation, data retention, and researcher-role procedures before collection.

### 5. Generate data without leading participants

Ask for a concrete recent episode and reconstruct its timeline. Probe what happened before, during, and after the focal decision; alternatives considered; constraints; other actors; trade-offs; emotions; social exposure; workarounds; non-action; and actual subsequent use.

Avoid asking participants to name their “job,” approve the researcher's job statement, design a product, or predict distant future behavior. Avoid presenting a proposed solution before learning the current story unless the study explicitly tests a later-stage hypothesis.

Separate field notes into observation, participant account, researcher interpretation, and follow-up questions.

### 6. Analyze in evidence-linked stages

1. Familiarize with the corpus and create case summaries.
2. Segment by job episode rather than arbitrary transcript length.
3. Code close to the data before applying higher-order JTBD constructs.
4. Build a case-by-construct matrix and a timeline for each focal episode.
5. Compare within and across cases to identify recurring circumstances, progress patterns, forces, outcomes, and stakeholder tensions.
6. Form candidate job statements and retain alternative formulations.
7. Search actively for negative cases, rival explanations, and boundary conditions.
8. Distinguish evidence strength: direct account, observed behavior, documentary trace, or researcher inference.
9. If using ODI, stabilize the job map and outcome statements before fielding importance/satisfaction ratings.
10. Revise the construct map and document every material analytic decision.

Copy [jtbd-case-evidence-template.csv](assets/jtbd-case-evidence-template.csv) and [jtbd-codebook-template.csv](assets/jtbd-codebook-template.csv) when structured evidence tables help. Run `scripts/validate_evidence_table.py` on the case-evidence CSV before reporting findings.

### 7. Quantify only with an explicit design

Treat ODI scoring as an optional mixed-method module, not an automatic JTBD requirement. Use [odi-outcomes-template.csv](assets/odi-outcomes-template.csv) and `scripts/calculate_odi_scores.py` only after defining the rating scale, sampling frame, outcome wording, missing-data rules, and analysis population.

Report the formula, scale transformation, threshold, sample size, uncertainty, and sensitivity analysis. With importance and satisfaction on 0–10, the opportunity score ranges from 0 to 20; the underserved threshold of 10 sits inside that range rather than at its top. Treat that threshold as a source-specific heuristic, not a universal law or proof of market success.

### 8. Produce the requested artifact

For a thesis-oriented request, offer the smallest complete set appropriate to the stage:

- Theory positioning and variant rationale.
- Research questions and construct table.
- Sampling and recruitment plan.
- Interview/observation/diary instrument.
- Codebook and evidence matrix.
- Within-case and cross-case findings.
- Job statements, forces, outcomes, or stakeholder map.
- Method, findings, discussion, limitations, and reflexivity text.
- Source ledger and audit trail.

Label illustrative examples as hypothetical. Never invent participants, quotations, statistics, citations, page numbers, or saturation claims.

## Enforce quality gates

The list below is the working enforcement list. For the auditable version — every gate with an evidence-location column, grouped by stage, and covering the quantitative and reporting-standard checks omitted here — copy [jtbd-quality-gate-checklist.md](assets/jtbd-quality-gate-checklist.md) and attach the completed file to the audit trail.

Do not finalize until all applicable checks pass:

- **Variant integrity:** Name the primary formulation and keep incompatible constructs separate. Report adjacent approaches separately from it.
- **Method named, not lens:** Report the actual analytic procedure. "Analyzed using JTBD" names a lens and is never sufficient.
- **Construct validity:** Distinguish job, circumstance, need, outcome, solution, feature, activity, journey stage, and stakeholder role.
- **Episode evidence:** Anchor every core interpretation in one or more identifiable episodes.
- **Solution neutrality:** State jobs and outcomes without embedding the focal product.
- **Temporal evidence:** Reconstruct triggers, consideration, choice, use, and aftermath when explaining switching.
- **Competing explanations:** Consider price, access, habit, institutional rules, social norms, and researcher prompting.
- **Negative cases:** Report exceptions and revise scope conditions.
- **Traceability:** Link each finding to case IDs and source locators without exposing identities.
- **Claims discipline:** Separate source claims, empirical findings, interpretation, and design recommendation.
- **Method coherence:** Do not claim statistical generalization from qualitative samples or causal effects from retrospective narratives.
- **Ethics:** Minimize identifiable and sensitive detail, especially in health and workplace research.
- **Citation discipline:** Verify bibliographic facts and page anchors against the actual source; paraphrase copyrighted works and quote sparingly.

## Use terminology consistently

The bilingual glossary in [theory-and-variants.md](references/theory-and-variants.md) section 8 is the single source of truth. Read it before producing Traditional Chinese output, and do not maintain a competing list here or in study documents.

Four terms are mistranslated most often, so check them first:

- **job → 用途／待完成任務**, never 工作 when that would imply employment.
- **progress → 所欲進展**, distinct from **desired outcome → 期望成果**. The first is the change sought; the second is the metric for judging it.
- **circumstance → 情境**, which must carry concrete context and constraint, not a demographic label.
- **hire / fire → 雇用／解雇（方案）**, flagged as a metaphor at first use.

State that terminology is an analytic choice, then use it consistently throughout one study.
