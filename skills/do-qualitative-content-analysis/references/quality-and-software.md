# Quality, Documentation, and Software

## Internal Quality Checklist

Internal quality covers the data as well as the analysis. Work through both parts.

### Part A: Data Collection and Transcription

- How was the material recorded?
- Was a postscript prepared describing the interview or group situation, and when?
- Was the material transcribed in full, or selectively by an explicit rule?
- Which transcription rules were followed, and will they be disclosed?
- Who transcribed, and was transcription software involved?
- Was the transcript proof-read against the recording?
- Has the material been anonymized, and how?
- Can the recording still be synchronized with the transcript for re-checking?

See [transcription-and-anonymization.md](transcription-and-anonymization.md).

### Part B: Implementation of the Analysis

- Research questions guide category construction and post-coding analysis.
- Units of coding and context are explicit.
- Categories are defined, differentiated, and supported by examples.
- Deductive categories were tested against data.
- Inductive development is traceable.
- Pilot coding led to documented revisions.
- Recoding followed material category changes.
- Cases remain visible in category analysis.
- Negative, ambiguous, and boundary cases are analyzed.
- The analysis takes the whole dataset into account rather than a subset of striking passages.
- The number of coding passes is recorded, and every assignment is traceable to its passage.
- Quotations were selected by a stated rule that admits contradictions and counter-examples, not only plausible illustrations.
- Claims are linked to coded passages and case summaries.
- Counts, if used, have meaningful denominators and do not replace interpretation.
- The report documents software, teamwork, versions, and decisions.

## Intercoder Comparison

Use comparison when the study's team model and quality aims make it relevant.

1. Select varied material and categories.
2. Code independently using the same version of the guide.
3. Compare both segmentation and category decisions.
4. Diagnose whether differences arise from definition, context, training, or legitimate interpretation.
5. Revise definitions and anchors.
6. Recode affected material.
7. Report the procedure and any statistic in context.

Agreement does not prove category validity or interpretive quality.

## Ethics During Analysis

Anonymizing the transcripts does not settle the matter; see [transcription-and-anonymization.md](transcription-and-anonymization.md) for that stage. Three exposures are specific to this method:

- **Case summaries and case profiles reassemble the person.** Each is a condensed portrait of one participant across every category. Details that are individually harmless become identifying once gathered into one paragraph, particularly in a small organization or a single ward.
- **Evaluative categories assign a judgement.** Recording that a named case shows a "low" level of something is an assessment made against the researcher's standard, not a neutral description. Say whose standard it is, and consider what follows if the case is recognized.
- **Cross-tabs with small cells identify people.** A cell containing one case is not anonymous because the name was removed.

For the general framework of analysis-stage ethics, see the ethics section in `analyze-qualitative-data-with-displays`.

## Journal Reporting Checklists

Where the corpus came from interviews or focus groups and the target is a health journal, COREQ will usually be required; SRQR is the broader alternative. Neither replaces the documentation this method demands of itself — category development, coding-guide versions, and post-coding analysis. Complete the checklist, and where an item assumes a design you did not use, state what you did instead rather than leaving the cell blank or claiming compliance you cannot evidence. See the reporting-guideline section in `design-qualitative-study`.

## Validity Checks

- compare categories with source context and case summaries;
- examine negative and deviant cases;
- test alternative category boundaries;
- seek expert, participant, or stakeholder input only where it can address a specific weakness;
- compare with theory or another data source without forcing convergence;
- assess transfer conditions and claim boundaries.

## QDA Software

Use software for:

- import, transcription linkage, anonymization, and organization;
- comments, memos, highlighting, and case summaries;
- category-system versions and coding;
- retrieval, summary grids, matrices, cross-tabs, and visualizations;
- team comparison and documentation;
- export of evidence and reports.

Do not let software folder structures dictate the category system. Invoke `use-nvivo-for-qualitative-analysis` for NVivo-specific implementation.
